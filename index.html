A calculator from Stairs to the Moon

Can your organizing model grow big enough to win?

Most base-building organizations run on paid staff, funded by foundations and donors. That works at small scale. But if each member costs more to organize than they contribute, every new member widens the gap, and the outside money needed grows right along with you.

This tool shows where that limit is for your model. It takes about three minutes, and you only need two numbers to start: roughly how many members your organization has, and how many staff.

Sources and assumptions
  • Household income: U.S. Census Bureau, Income in the United States: 2025 (P60-289, September 2026). U.S. median $87,460; 10th percentile $20,010. Medians by race and Hispanic origin from the same report. "Lower-income" is two-thirds of the U.S. median, a common cutoff (used, for example, by Pew Research Center).
  • State medians: Census Bureau Current Population Survey, 2025, via FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis). Single-year state estimates are noisy; treat them as approximate.
  • Population: Census Bureau Vintage 2025 estimates (July 1, 2025). U.S. total 341,784,857. Average congressional district: 761,169 people, from the 2020 apportionment.
  • Unions: Bureau of Labor Statistics, Union Members, 2025 (10.0% of wage and salary workers). 1954 peak: about 35% of wage and salary workers (28.3% of all employed workers, per Congressional Research Service report RL32553).
  • Dues benchmarks: union dues commonly run about 1 to 2% of pay; a traditional tithe is 10%.
  • Democracy philanthropy: $6.7 billion in 2025, per the U.S. Democracy Hub as reported by the Chronicle of Philanthropy. This covers all democracy and civic work (voting, journalism, litigation, research, bridge-building, organizing) from funders across the political spectrum, so it overstates what's available for organizing.
  • All U.S. foundation giving: $117.15 billion in 2025, per Giving USA 2026.
  • "Your share" of democracy philanthropy divides the national total by population. Real funding isn't distributed evenly.
  • "What you raise today" assumes your current gap (staff cost minus dues) is covered by grants and donors. If you have other revenue, such as events or earned income, your outside funding is smaller. The model counts staff costs only, not rent, travel, or other expenses, so real gaps are larger.
